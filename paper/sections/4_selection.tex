\section{Errors follow wrong choices, not failed reading}
\label{sec:selection}

To locate the loss we replayed the full held-out split (2{,}000 sequences, 256{,}000 queries) through all twelve
selector checkpoints of Section~\ref{sec:budget}, using each run's own numerics and data, and recorded for each query
whether the \emph{retrieval layer} (layer 1, the second of each model's two layers) selected the super-block and the
fine block holding the queried key. Every replay first
had to reproduce its run's recorded held-out accuracy, loss and split fingerprint; all twelve matched the split
fingerprint exactly, the accuracy to within one query in 256{,}000 and the loss to within $3{\times}10^{-6}$. The
analysis is descriptive and post hoc. Some finer probes below use earlier checkpoints from the M4 profile, labelled
as such (Appendix~\ref{app:runs}, Table~\ref{tab:selection_m4}).

\paragraph{Reading is not the bottleneck at $K{=}4$.} For all eleven models that learned, recall was 0.978--1.000
whenever the retrieval layer selected the key's block (Table~\ref{tab:selection}). On the M4-profile checkpoints it was
0.997--1.000 (Table~\ref{tab:selection_m4}), or 0.993--1.000 including the repair arms of Section~\ref{sec:fixes}; on a
64-sequence probe of those checkpoints, ablating the retrieval layer's fine branch dropped it to 0.04--0.08 for the
baseline models, so correct answers come from reading the selected tokens. The bottleneck is finding the block.

\paragraph{$S_1{=}6$ makes fewer wrong choices than $S_1{=}2$ at both levels.} Although every key lies in super-blocks
0 and 1, the $S_1{=}2$ models missed the key's super-block on 4.5--14.1\% of queries (19.1\% for the run that never
learned). From $S_1{=}4$ upward (one seed at $S_1{=}4$) such parent misses fell to at most 0.4\%. Fine-level choices
improved too; we call a query a \emph{fine-level miss} when the key's super-block was selected but the key's block
was not. Paired on the same seed, the key-block hit rate rose from 0.463 to 0.962 (seed 123), from 0.570 to 0.933
(810001) and from 0.526 to 0.845 (810002); parent misses fell from 0.13--0.14 to zero and fine-level misses from
0.30--0.40 to 0.04--0.16. This is an accounting of where the misses occur, not a causal explanation. Flat selection
($S_1{=}12$, whose parent hit is 1 by construction) chose the key's block less often than $S_1{=}6$ on the pilot seed
(0.846 against 0.962), all of the difference at the fine level, where it chooses among all past blocks rather than
up to 66 candidates. That is one seed.

\paragraph{Recovering from a wrong choice is a seed-level property.} Recall after a key-block miss ranged from 0.002 to
0.781 across models, with no pattern by budget (Table~\ref{tab:selection}); on seed 810002, for example, answers after
a miss add about 0.10 to the $S_1{=}6$ model's recall. For the $S_1{=}2$ pilot model, recall after a miss fell with the
distance from the key's block to the nearest selected block at or after it (0.89 at one block, 0.61 at two, 0.21 at
three to eight), consistent with reading neighbouring blocks; but 20.5\% of its queries had no selected block from the
key's block to eight blocks after it, and 26\% of those were still answered correctly, so some answers come by
another route. On the M4-profile $S_1{=}2$ checkpoint (64-sequence probe), ablating the retrieval layer's coarse
branch left such distant-miss answers unchanged (0.382), while ablating its fine branch removed most of them
(0.055); this is consistent with the first layer copying key information into the blocks that were selected, which
we did not test directly.

\paragraph{The parent level can be solved by position.} In this MQAR layout every key lies in the first 256 tokens,
i.e.\ in super-blocks 0 and 1. A selector can therefore reach a high parent hit rate by preferring early
super-blocks instead of matching content: on the M4 profile, for queries at positions of 528 or more, 92.8\% of the
parent choices of the combined router-gate and max-pool arm of Section~\ref{sec:fixes} were exactly super-blocks
$\{0, 1\}$. \textbf{Conclusions about the parent budget $S_1$ may not transfer to tasks whose keys are spread across
the context.} We return to this in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\paragraph{The fine budget matters more than the parent budget, post hoc.} Changing the budgets of an M4-profile
checkpoint at inference only, on a 64-sequence subset (8{,}192 queries; hence 0.580 here against the full-split 0.594 in
Table~\ref{tab:selection_m4}), the key-block hit rate of the $S_1{=}2$ seed-123 model rose from 0.580 at $K{=}4$ to 0.769 at
$K{=}8$ and 0.884 at $K{=}16$, but only to 0.614 and 0.604 at $S_1{=}4$ and $S_1{=}6$ with $K{=}4$. Models trained at
$K{=}4$ would learn different scores at other budgets, so this is suggestive only; all trained results in this paper use
$K{=}4$.
